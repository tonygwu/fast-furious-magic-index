- characters: Dominic Toretto (occupant/survivor — shackled inside the bus), Brian O'Conner (driver — rams the bus in Dom's Charger), Mia Toretto (driver — a second car used to force the bus off line)

- inferred: The exact number of rolls and the fate of the bus driver/guards are not detailed in the plot sources; presumed survivable off-screen since the film treats it as a clean getaway, not a mass-casualty event (inferred by this researcher from the absence of any death/injury being mentioned for bus personnel).

### ff05-c03 Corvette canyon dive
- sequence: train heist escape (immediately follows the fight on the train)
- approx_time: 27–29 min (ESTIMATE)
- characters: Dominic Toretto (driver/survivor), Brian O'Conner (occupant/survivor)
- depicted: With no other escape route off the train/road, Dom drives the stolen Corvette off a canyon edge with Brian aboard; the car falls, and the two are shown swimming to the surface afterward with no depicted serious injury.
- inferred: How exactly Brian ends up in the car (whether he jumps from the moving train onto it, as one retrospective states, or is already inside) is described slightly differently across the sources found; treated as a minor disagreement (see below). What happens between the two men reaching the shore and their later on-screen capture by Reyes's men (the very next plot beat per Wikipedia/Fandom) is not depicted or described in any source found — this is a real gap in the walkthrough, not filled in here (see Open Questions).
- real_world_params: canyon height as depicted (post-VFX) = approx. 300 ft (https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/mpc-on-track-with-fast-five/, quoting VFX supervisor Guillaume Rocheron of MPC — analysis/production account, i.e. what the finished shot shows, not a number stated as dialogue in the film); real filming cliff height = approx. 50 ft (same source, location — a quarry, reported elsewhere as being near Atlanta, Georgia, though that specific detail came only from an unverified searching synthesis and is not independently confirmed here); on-screen freefall duration = approx. 15 seconds (https://www.inverse.com/article/28298-fast-furious-five-freefall-retrospective, film); comparable Corvette (C6-generation base model) curb weight = 3,273 lb / 1,485 kg (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvette_(C6) — this is a proxy for a comparable real chassis, not a confirmed weight for the specific in-universe 1963–66 Grand Sport replica body built for filming).
- sources: https://screenrant.com/fast-5-dom-brian-cliff-freefall-filmed-how/, https://www.inverse.com/article/28298-fast-furious-five-freefall-retrospective, https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/mpc-on-track-with-fast-five/
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: Inverse's account implies Brian jumps onto Dom's moving car from the train just before the drop ("Brian jumps onto Dom's car and the two fall"); ScreenRant's account instead frames it as Dom driving "to rescue Brian from the train" without describing a mid-air jump onto the vehicle. Both describe the same outcome (both men in the car, off the cliff, 15-second fall) but differ on the exact choreography of how Brian got into/onto the car. Flagged for the analyst since it could matter for a physics estimate of the jump itself.

### ff05-c06 Vault extraction from the police station
- sequence: vault heist finale
- approx_time: 100–104 min (ESTIMATE)
- characters: Luke Hobbs (driver/performer — Gurkha), Elena Neves (occupant/performer), Dominic Toretto (driver — one Charger), Brian O'Conner (driver — other Charger)
- depicted: Hobbs and Elena drive the Gurkha LAPV through the wall of the police station to breach the vault chamber and provide covering fire while Dom and Brian attach cables/harnesses from their two Dodge Chargers to the evidence vault holding Reyes's cash, then physically tear the vault out of the building.
- inferred: The exact attachment method (chain vs. cable, how it survives the initial wrench of ripping a vault from reinforced construction) is not itemized beyond "harnesses"/cables in the sources found.
- real_world_params: vault — real prop weight used for the physical drag stunt = 9,000 lb (https://nofilmschool.com/fast-five-justin-lin-vault-stunts and corroborated by https://www.avclub.com/take-a-moment-to-appreciate-all-the-work-that-went-into-1847026478, real prop/production fact); depicted/in-story vault mass has been separately estimated at roughly 100,000 lb / 45,359 kg by online physics write-ups (analysis, not stated in the film — this figure came from a general web-search synthesis of a physicsforums.com thread and a "Reel Physics" YouTube breakdown, and could not be independently re-verified by direct fetch before the search budget ran out); vault height = approx. 8 ft (nofilmschool, production fact — "six versions of the 8-foot high vault were built"); connecting cables = 30 ft (nofilmschool, production fact); vehicles used to pull it = two 2010 Dodge Charger police cars, 368–370 hp, 395 lb-ft torque, curb weight approx. 4,021–4,530 lb for the seventh-generation Pursuit package (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Charger_(LX), real vehicle spec, film uses this exact model per Wikipedia's plot text); breach vehicle = Gurkha LAPV, specs as in ff05-c05.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Five (plot and production sections), https://fastandfurious.fandom.com/wiki/Vault_Heist (via a reader proxy), https://nofilmschool.com/fast-five-justin-lin-vault-stunts
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: the two "real-world weight" figures above (9,000 lb prop vs. ~100,000 lb depicted/analyzed) are not in conflict — one is what was physically towed during filming, the other is an outside estimate of how much steel the on-screen vault "should" weigh given its shown dimensions. Both are given so the analyst can choose which is relevant to the physics question being asked.

### ff05-c09 Vault swap into a garbage truck, undetected
- sequence: vault heist finale
- approx_time: within 104–118 min, exact moment unknown (ESTIMATE)
- characters: Gisele Yashar (performer), Santos (performer), Leo (performer)
- depicted: During the chase, the real vault is secretly transferred into a disguised garbage truck and replaced with a decoy before the group reaches the bridge; Hobbs later finds the vault at the bridge is empty and realizes the switch happened, but the crew has already gone.
- inferred: The mechanical means of uncoupling a vault under tow from two moving Chargers and coupling it into a garbage truck's body, mid-chase, without any of the many pursuing police units or Hobbs noticing, is not shown or described by any source found — this is the single largest logistics gap in the finale and is noted for the analyst as a candidate that straddles "physical stunt" and "plot armor" (see notes).
- real_world_params: substitute vehicle = a Mack MRU-model garbage truck (https://fastandfurious.fandom.com/wiki/Vault_Heist via a reader proxy, film); no further physical parameters (speeds, distances, time available for the swap) are given by any source found.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Five (plot: "Dom's crew switched it via a trash truck before they reached the bridge"), https://fastandfurious.fandom.com/wiki/Vault_Heist (via a reader proxy)
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: this item is filed as a physical-stunt candidate (a covert heavy-load transfer mid-chase) rather than in Plot Armor, on the judgment call that its improbability is primarily mechanical/logistical (could a stunt team really re-rig a multi-ton load between two vehicles, unnoticed, inside a fast-moving chase) rather than purely narrative. A reasonable analyst could instead classify it as "implausible access/deception" and move it to Plot Armor; flagging that choice explicitly rather than silently picking one.

### ff05-p01 Letty Ortiz alive
- characters: Letty Ortiz (subject, shown only in a photograph), Luke Hobbs, Monica Fuentes

### ff05-p02 GT40 chip as a complete criminal ledger
- characters: Brian O'Conner (discoverer), Vince (attempted thief of the chip), Hernan Reyes (subject of the data)
- what: info magic — a single computer chip recovered from a car conveniently contains not just some data but the full financial map of a drug lord's empire, including exact cash locations, sufficient to plan a $100 million heist
- explained_in_film: partly — it is explained narratively as DEA surveillance/evidence data, but the completeness and precision of the information (enough to know exactly where all the cash is kept) is a convenience not otherwise justified
- contradicts_prior_canon: no prior-canon contradiction; flagged purely for informational implausibility
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Five (plot: "the chip contains financial details of Reyes's criminal empire, including the locations of US$100 million in cash")

### ff05-p03 Handprint biometric bypass via seduction
- characters: Gisele Yashar (performer), Hernan Reyes (target), Han Lue (present)
- what: info magic / implausible access — Gisele obtains a usable handprint/biometric impression of Reyes through physical contact during a seduction, later used to defeat a biometric palm-scanner security system protecting his money
