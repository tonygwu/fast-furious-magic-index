### ff10-c04 The DM-79 "neutron mine" rolls uncontrolled through central Rome
- sequence: Rome mission (present day) — the film's marquee set piece
- approx_time: ~22–33 min (estimate)
- characters: Dominic Toretto (driver, pursuer), Roman Pearce (driver, pursuer), Tej Parker (occupant/performer), Han Lue (occupant/performer), Ramsey (occupant/performer), Dante Reyes (performer, remote — triggers/releases the device)
- depicted: Tej, Han, and Ramsey open the truck expecting a computer chip and instead find a large spherical device (fictional "DM-79 sub-nautical neutron mine"). Dante remotely arms and releases it into Rome's streets as a free-rolling giant ball, crushing cars and threatening bystanders as it careens through the city, including down the Spanish Steps, while Dom's team tries to steer it away from crowds.
- inferred: The device's real destructive yield is fictional/unstated; "neutron mine" is not a real ordnance class.
- real_world_params: Practical bomb prop = a "one-ton metal ball" per director Louis Leterrier's own description, rigged on an axle with cables to two overhead lines (one forward, one back) driven by a glider-tow winch; testing reached about 35 mph and the rig could start/stop it anywhere (source: stunt coordinator Andy/Jack Gill interview via High On Films). The same article's own framing elsewhere calls it "a 10-ton bomb" — this is very likely the in-story/dramatic description of the fictional device's weight, not the physical prop's weight, but no source explicitly reconciles the two figures. Sequence = 554 shots per IMDb news coverage. Filming locations for the chase: Via Cristoforo Colombo, Via dei Fori Imperiali, Ponte Umberto I, and the Spanish Steps in Rome, plus additional plates from Turin and Genzano, per ScreenRant's locations piece; Rome's city government closed streets for the shoot per the IndieWire/IMDb coverage.
- sources: https://www.highonfilms.com/fast-x-giant-bomb-rolling-scene/, https://m.imdb.com/news/ni64092646/, https://screenrant.com/fast-x-map-filming-locations/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_X (Plot: "Dante remotely activates it and releases it into Rome's streets")
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: Bomb weight is genuinely ambiguous between "one-ton" (director's own words, describing the practical prop) and "10-ton" (a descriptive figure used in at least one article's own framing, source of the figure itself uncited). Flagged for the analyst rather than resolved — the two numbers matter enormously for momentum/impact-force calculations, and it is unclear whether "10-ton" refers to a claimed in-story fictional mass or is simply an inflated journalistic rounding.
