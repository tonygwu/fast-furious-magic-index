### ff06-c12 Runway chase: cars vs. taking-off cargo plane
- sequence: SP-5, airfield/plane finale
- approx_time: approx 104–117 min (movie-censorship.com's last timecode cluster in this sequence runs 1:48:04–1:51:00, i.e. within this window, for the hand-to-hand fighting portion; the runway-length physics writeups describe a "13-minute" — or per one source "15-minute" — sequence overall)
- characters: Dominic Toretto (driver), Brian O'Conner (driver), Roman Pearce (driver), Tej Parker (driver/support), Han Lue (occupant), Gisele Yashar (occupant), Letty Ortiz (driver/performer), Luke Hobbs (performer), Owen Shaw (driver/beneficiary), Vegh, Klaus, Adolfson and other named mercenary crew (performers)
- depicted: The team chases Shaw's crew's cargo plane down the runway in vehicles as it accelerates for takeoff, eventually using grappling cables/hooks to anchor it and prevent liftoff, all while it is still moving at high speed.
- inferred: none beyond the above sequencing.
- real_world_params:
  - Plane model per the film's own plot description = Antonov An-124 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious_6, Plot section, confirmed verbatim on request: "Antonov An-124"). Real An-124 specs (location/real-world spec, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-124_Ruslan): max takeoff weight 402,000 kg; length 69.1 m; wingspan 73.3 m; takeoff run required 3,000 m (9,800 ft); 4 engines, 226.8 kN (51,000 lbf) thrust each.
  - However, the published physics teardowns' total-thrust figure (see below) matches a 6-engine aircraft, not the 4-engine An-124 — see disagreement note.
  - Car speed needed to reach the plane at the start of the sequence = 115 mph; plane's stated takeoff speed = 172 mph (film-depicted figures per ScreenRant's recap, https://screenrant.com/fast-furious-6-runway-length/, itself citing a Vulture second-by-second breakdown).
  - Runway length implied by the depicted chase timing: 26 miles per screenwriter Chris Morgan's own on-record estimate ("Technically for the plane to be in the air for the duration of that set piece, the runway would have to be 26 miles long," https://www.cbr.com/fast-6-chris-morgan-26-mile-runway/, analysis/writer's-own-math); >28 miles per Vulture's independent breakdown (via ScreenRant, analysis); 28.829 miles per Empire's calculation with aeronautics engineer Gary Spoors of GJD Aerotech (via https://www.aviationpros.com/gse/baggage-cargo/blog/11281633/how-long-was-the-runway-in-fast-and-furious-6, analysis; I could not read Empire's own page text directly — its numeric payoff is rendered as images); approx. 30 nautical miles (~48 km) per "Dan's Reality Check" allowing for deceleration (https://supermarcey.com/2013/06/26/dans-reality-check-fast-and-furious-6/, analysis). All four independent estimates agree on the general order of magnitude (26–30 miles) despite different methods.
  - Sequence duration: 13 minutes per ScreenRant/aviationpros; 15 minutes per Dan's Reality Check (minor disagreement between two independent sources, both analysis-tagged, not film-stated).
  - Real-world longest paved runway for comparison: 3.4 miles per ScreenRant/aviationpros; 5.5 km (≈3.4 mi) per Dan's Reality Check — these two figures agree once converted.
  - Total engine thrust used in the physics argument: 312,000 lbf across six engines per Dan's Reality Check (analysis). This matches the real An-225's 6 × 51,600 lbf ≈ 309,600 lbf (location/real-world spec, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-225_Mriya), not the 4-engine An-124 that Wikipedia's plot text names.
  - Real filming location for the finale's plane-crash beats: RAF Bovingdon, Hertfordshire (film-production fact, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious_6 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Bovingdon). That airfield's longest original runway was 1,634 yd (1,494 m); today only a 650 m strip remains usable (location/real-world spec, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Bovingdon) — around 1% of the ~44–48 km the physics analyses say the depicted chase would require.
  - fxguide's VFX breakdown (https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/fast-furious-6-just-plane-crazy/) independently states production referred to the aircraft simply as "an Antonov" and built practical fuselage sections at 1:1 scale (75 ft long body section, ~100 ft tail/ramp section), consistent with modeling the larger An-225 rather than the smaller An-124.
