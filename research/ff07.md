### ff07-c13 Etihad Towers: Lykan HyperSport jumps between three skyscrapers
- sequence: Act 2 climax, Abu Dhabi
- approx_time: unknown
- characters: Dominic Toretto (role: driver/performer), Brian O'Conner (role: occupant/performer)
- depicted: Dom and Brian steal a Lykan HyperSport (containing the God's Eye flash drive) from around the 45th-50th floor of one Etihad Tower, then drive it out through the glass façade, across open air, and into a second tower; they repeat the jump into a third tower, then bail out on foot inside that building (destroying terracotta-warrior statues in an art gallery) just before the now-driverless car flies out again and falls to the ground.
- inferred: Both men surviving unhurt is directly shown; the "art gallery" detail comes from one source and was not independently cross-checked against a second full account.
- real_world_params: starting floor = reported as "about the 45th floor" by one source and "near the 50th floor" by another (disagreement, film); real height of the tallest Etihad Tower (Tower 2) = 305.3 m / 1,002 ft (Etihad Towers Wikipedia page, location); real height of the shortest tower (Tower 5) = 217.5 m / 714 ft (same source, location); estimated gap between towers used for physics calc by a consulted physicist = 140-170 ft, ~150 ft assumed (Slate article, analysis); fall distance for the final (third) jump = described as "a two-story fall" (~The National, film/analysis mix); minimum speed for a 2-story fall per the physicist = ≥100 mph; minimum speed for a 4-story fall = ≥70 mph (Slate/Prof. Lee Loveridge, analysis); distance needed to reach 100 mph = roughly 300+ ft at constant acceleration, or 275-750 ft at constant power depending on target speed (Slate, analysis); Lykan HyperSport curb weight = 1,380 kg / 3,042 lb (W Motors Lykan HyperSport Wikipedia page, real spec) — a Slate source separately estimated "3,000+ lb, ~3,400 lb with occupants"; Lykan HyperSport claimed power = 552 kW / 740 hp at 7,100 rpm, manufacturer claim, independently unverified (Wikipedia); Lykan HyperSport claimed top speed = 395 km/h / 245 mph, manufacturer claim (Wikipedia); Lykan HyperSport claimed 0-60 mph = under 3 seconds (Slate); real-world unit price = $3.4 million (Wikipedia, Slate)
- sources: https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts/furious-7-etihad-towers-stunt-can-it-be-done-1.53542 ; https://slate.com/culture/2015/04/furious-7-skyscraper-jump-could-it-really-happen-we-asked-a-physicist.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Towers ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_Motors_Lykan_HyperSport ; https://screenrant.com/fast-furious-7-etihad-towers-skyscraper-stunt-shot/
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: (1) Starting floor disagreement: ~45th floor (The National) vs. ~50th floor (a separate aggregated search result). (2) Physicist Prof. Lee Loveridge's published conclusion was that this is "probably the most plausible stunt in the whole movie," though he flagged the car's suspension stiffness and in-building turning radius as implausible — recorded here as source data per the brief's instruction to collect existing published analyses, not as this researcher's own scoring. (3) Behind-the-scenes constraint: despite the real Etihad Towers appearing in exterior establishing shots, the actual jump-through-glass stunt was filmed on an Atlanta soundstage using 40-ft-tall built facades, and none of the ten Lykan HyperSport replicas built for the film were the real $3.4M carbon-fiber car (they were cheaper fiberglass-bodied purpose-builds; 9 of 10 were destroyed) — screenrant, Wikipedia.
