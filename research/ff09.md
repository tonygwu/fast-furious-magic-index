### ff09-c14 THE SPACE SEQUENCE — Pontiac Fiero rocket car destroys the satellite
- sequence: Tbilisi finale, concurrent/interleaved with c11–c13 (exact interleaving vs. sequential placement is uncertain — see notes)
- approx_time: ~112–130 min (estimate; likely cross-cut with the ground chase rather than a single contiguous block)
- characters: Tej Parker (driver/performer/survivor), Roman Pearce (driver/performer/survivor), Sean Boswell (support, pilots the carrier plane), Earl Hu (support, pilots/operates systems on the carrier plane)

**depicted — launch method:** Sean and Earl fly a cargo/carrier plane, with the modified Pontiac Fiero mounted on/attached to it, up to an altitude reported consistently across three independent sources as **50,000 feet** (≈15.2 km). At that altitude the plane releases/ejects the car into a brief free-fall, after which the car's own rocket propulsion ignites and it climbs the rest of the way into orbit under its own power. Sources: [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/fast-furious-9-plot-how-f9-takes-space-ludacris-tyrese-gibson-1604057), [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/f9-space-scene-science-why-how-realistic/), and a direct page fetches-derived summary of the film's own dialogue transcript (medium confidence — see caveat above) which additionally frames it as a "German airbase" launch, consistent with the rocket car having been built in Germany earlier in the film.

**depicted — engine/fuel:** Described on screen (per the transcript-summary) as a "rocket engine" fitted to the Fiero, incorporating a nitrous-oxide tank and a "LOX line" (liquid-oxygen feed) with a "quick-disconnect valve," plus a "ceramic polymer coating" on the vehicle described as making it "100% fireproof" for heat protection. The ceramic-coating detail is corroborated by an independent pre-release trailer-analysis source ([Screen Rant, rocket car explained](https://screenrant.com/fast-furious-9-rocket-car-explained/)), which also describes three visible rocket nozzles and two fins bolted to the car body, and notes the car's silver interior insulation. No specific engine model or precise thrust figure is given anywhere.

**depicted — altitude/orbit reached:** No specific orbital altitude or apogee number is stated on screen in any source found. The team's stated destination/return point is "the International Space Station" (per Wikipedia's plot summary: "Tej and Roman reach the International Space Station and return safely to Earth"), which in the real world orbits at ≈400–422 km — but the film does not itself state a number; this is an inference from the ISS being visually reached, not a quoted figure. One outlet ([Space.com/Yahoo syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/f9-took-car-space-5-150000973.html)) describes the altitude only as "way more than a quarter mile straight up," which reads as a drag-racing-culture joke about distance (a quarter mile is the standard drag-strip length) rather than a literal claimed altitude.

**depicted — suits:** Both men wear improvised, non-professional spacesuits. Independent sources converge on describing these as: full-body suits resembling wetsuits/rubber diving suits ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2021/06/fast-furious-9-space-car-astronaut-interview.html), [AVClub](https://www.avclub.com/f9-got-audiences-back-to-theaters-by-giving-them-all-th-1848010309): "rubber diving costumes"), described elsewhere as makeshift EMU-style ("extravehicular mobility unit") suits with scuba-style helmets substituting for real breathing gear, held together visibly with **duct tape** ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/fast-furious-9-plot-how-f9-takes-space-ludacris-tyrese-gibson-1604057), [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/f9-space-scene-science-why-how-realistic/), independently corroborated by [gonewiththetwins.com](https://gonewiththetwins.com/f9-the-fast-saga-2021/): "spacesuits duct-taped together"). The transcript-derived summary separately describes the suits as accounting for "pressure differential," implying at least a stated in-universe rationale for pressurization, though not a real spacesuit's construction.

**depicted — how they hit the satellite:** The plan is to use a powerful electromagnet mounted on the front of the car to fry/short-circuit the satellite's electronics from close range or contact. This fails — per the transcript-derived summary, the stress/pressure of the launch is said to have jammed the magnet's power cable/connection. As a fallback, Roman and Tej instead **ram/crash the car directly into the satellite**, physically destroying it by collision, which stops the Project Aries upload. (Sources: [Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2021/06/fast-furious-9-space-car-astronaut-interview.html): "Initially, they attempt to disable the satellite using a powerful electromagnet mounted on the front... When this fails, they just crash the car into the satellite"; corroborated by the transcript-derived summary and by [themoviespoiler.com](https://themoviespoiler.com/movies/f9/): "Tej and Roman's car fails, forcing them to ram into the satellite in order to destroy it.")

**depicted — how they return:** This is the least-depicted part of the sequence. Per the Slate interview and its author's own description of re-watching the scene, the film does not show a technical re-entry: after destroying the satellite, the astronauts are shown "still in some kind of orbit," the film "sort of glosses over" the re-entry plan, they are shown "coast[ing] over to a space station" and waving at it — and the very next shot is them safely on Earth. Wikipedia's plot summary states simply "Tej and Roman reach the International Space Station and return safely to Earth," with no depicted mechanism (no parachute, no capsule, no splashdown, and no visible spacecraft to dock with the ISS is described by any source). I could not find any source describing an on-screen re-entry, heat-shield use, parachute deployment, or landing for the Fiero itself — treat "how they return" as **effectively undepicted / hand-waved off screen**, which is itself a notable finding for the analyst (there may be no physical mechanism to model at all for the return leg, only a hard cut to safety).
