# 2 Fast 2 Furious (2003) — film_id ff02

> **Provenance.** Research notes for one film, compiled from public sources and written for the analyst who

Runtime: 107 minutes, US theatrical cut (Wikipedia; matches MPAA/theatrical listings: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Fast_2_Furious). UK BBFC recorded two different figures that are NOT simply "cut vs uncut" content: UK cinema release 107m31s (12A, with 3 kicks/a stamp/a spit trimmed from one fight) and UK home-video release 103m8s (15, uncut, i.e. MORE violent content but a SHORTER time) — the shorter-but-fuller home-video figure is best explained by PAL frame-rate speed-up (25fps vs 24fps), not missing footage (https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/2-fast-2-furious-q29sbgvjdglvbjpwwc00ntu4oda). Treat "107 minutes" as the theatrical-cut anchor for this file.
Credits start approx 100–101 min (ESTIMATE — see Presence log; no chapter database found). Confirmed: the first ~3 minutes of the end credits are a 3D-animated sequence of the film's cars racing around a stylized Miami (no cast on screen), and there is no scene of any kind, mid- or post-credit, after that (https://mediastinger.com/2-fast-2-furious-2003-extras-during-the-credits/). Deleted scenes are out of scope per brief.

## Candidates

### ff02-c01 Opening race — canal-bridge jump (Brian, clean landing)
- sequence: opening street race ("Bridge Jump Scene", Movieclips 1/9)
- approx_time: ~8–13 min (estimate)
- characters: Brian O'Conner (driver, 1999 Nissan Skyline GT-R R34), Tej Parker (race organizer, not a driver in this race)
- depicted: Brian races three other street racers through Miami at night; partway through, a canal/drawbridge starts to rise; Brian hits his nitrous button and clears the jump, landing cleanly on all four tires past the far downslope, then wins the race with a drift across the finish line.
- inferred: the exact real-world bridge (name/location) is not identified by any source found; production notes tie the film generally to Miami Beach/Seven Mile Bridge/Homestead-area filming but do not tie this specific shot to a named span. The script's stage direction gives Brian's jump speed as "150" (unit not stated, presumably mph) — this is a screenplay number, not confirmed studio-released or reviewer-confirmed for the final cut.
- real_world_params: jump speed ≈150 mph (source: screenplay stage direction only, unconfirmed for final cut); 1999 Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 curb weight 1,560 kg / 3,439 lb, official power 206 kW/280 PS/276 hp @ 6,800 rpm (Japanese self-regulation rating; independent dyno tests cited by Wikipedia put real output closer to 327 hp), torque 260 lb-ft @ 4,400 rpm, 0–62 mph 4.4 s (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Skyline_GT-R, real-world spec); real bridge gap/height: unknown — searched the Yahoo Finance and DrivenCarGuide behind-the-scenes pieces and the S2ki slideshow (403, title only), none give a numeric jump distance or height, only anecdotal production process.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Fast_2_Furious (paraphrase: "a race... and a bridge jump"), screenplay (scenes 27–36), https://finance.yahoo.com/news/pulling-off-crazy-s2000-bridge-142200761.html, Movieclips title "Bridge Jump Scene (1/9)"
- sources_ok: true
- post_credit: false
- notes / disagreements: The bridge-jump stunt is also documented as involving a remote-control Honda S2000 (Suki's car, see ff02-c02's sibling detail below) — the Yahoo/DrivenCarGuide pieces describe an RC rig and a chase-vehicle brake failure that injured the RC operator, but don't say which specific car(s) in the scene used the RC rig versus a stunt driver.

## Rejected (considered, ordinary)
- Verone's backyard sprinklers switched on so he can talk without being overheard — a security-theater character beat, not a physical stunt.
- Rat-under-a-heated-bucket torture of Detective Whitworth — brutal (per BBFC content notes) but not a physically-improbable feat; director John Singleton says it's based on a real historical interrogation method (https://www.comingsoon.net/extras/news/190-john-singleton-on-2-fast-2-furious).
- Brian and Roman subduing Enrique bare-handed at the marina (the fight the BBFC/movie-censorship.com timecode 1:32:22 documents) — an ordinary action-movie fight resolution against one man; no extraordinary physical feat beyond genre norms is depicted.
- General police/henchman marksmanship failing to hit fleeing leads throughout every chase — ubiquitous action-movie convention, not scene-specific.
- Ordinary nitrous-boosted straight-line racing during the non-jump portions of every race — heightened but not physically extraordinary on its own.
- Verone's Ferrari being towed with a valuable ring left in the glovebox — a mundane plot contrivance (people do forget things in cars), not a stunt.

## Plot armor

### ff02-p01 Handheld "ESD" dart-gun that instantly fries a car's entire electrical system
- characters: Agent Markham (operator, capture scene); two unnamed officers (operator, later highway-chase reuse, screenplay only)
- what: institutional — federal/customs agents carry a rocket-launcher-shaped, single-shot device that fires a dart which sticks to a moving car and delivers "an electrical charge" that instantly kills the car's entire electrical system (engine, brakes, dash) from a single point of contact.
- explained_in_film: partly — it is shown and named informally ("ESD" in the screenplay) but never explained technically.
- contradicts_prior_canon: no (first appearance in the franchise as far as sources found show).
- sources: screenplay (scene 37, and scene 166 for reuse — reuse is screenplay-only/unconfirmed), https://slickdungeon.blog/2023/05/11/2-fast-2-furious-movie-review/ (independently describes "some kind of harpoon style weapon... delivers a sort of EMP jolt")

### ff02-p02 Verone runs instant law-enforcement-style background checks on Brian and Roman
- characters: Carter Verone, unnamed "tech"/whiz-kid
- what: implausible access/knowledge — a private criminal has on-demand access to what reads as a full law-enforcement rap sheet (juvenile record, prior arrests, prison time) for two men he just met, pulled up on a home computer within minutes.
- explained_in_film: no.
- contradicts_prior_canon: no.
- sources: screenplay (scenes 82, 84) only — single-sourced; not independently confirmed for the theatrical cut.

### ff02-p03 Full record wipe / pardon for Brian and Roman
- characters: Brian O'Conner, Roman Pearce, Agent Markham, Agent Bilkins
- what: pardons — a fugitive ex-cop (Brian) and a felon with an armed-robbery record (Roman) both get their entire criminal records cleared by federal/customs authorities in exchange for one undercover operation, with no further legal process shown.
- explained_in_film: yes — stated directly as the deal at the start and confirmed delivered at the end.
- contradicts_prior_canon: no — this is the film's own premise, but it is exactly the kind of institutional-magic resolution the brief asks to flag.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Fast_2_Furious ("Agent Markham clears both men's criminal records"), https://www.moviespoilers.io/movie/2-fast-2-furious ("granted their freedom and have their records cleared"), https://www.plotexplained.com/movie/2-fast-2-furious
